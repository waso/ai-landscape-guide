    Map 7 — Decision guide¶

    Prompt, RAG, fine-tune, or train?

    Start at the left column and move right only if the cheaper fix fails. Most real problems never leave the first column.

    Symptom First try (hours) Then (days) Last resort (weeks+) Don't
    Wrong, outdated or missing facts
    Doesn't know your product, your docs, last week's data    		 Put the facts in the prompt; add RAG or a search / DB tool Improve retrieval: chunking, hybrid search, reranker, citations Continued pre-training on your domain corpus (rare, expensive) Fine-tuning is a poor way to teach facts: it is unreliable and goes stale.
    Right content, wrong form
    Wrong tone, format, length, language; ignores conventions    		 Prompt engineering: explicit rules, few-shot examples, structured outputs LoRA fine-tune on a few hundred ideal examples Full fine-tune with thousands of examples Pre-training. Style is cheap to teach; knowledge is not.
    Can't do the task at all
    Fails at multi-step reasoning, complex code, long planning    		 Bigger or reasoning model; chain-of-thought; decompose into steps Agent with tools: let it run code, search, check its work RL fine-tuning with verifiable rewards on your task (research-grade) Expecting a 7B model to match a frontier model by prompting harder.
    Too slow or too expensive
    Latency, token bill, GPU cost    		 Shorter prompts, prompt caching, smaller model for easy requests (routing) Quantization, speculative decoding, better serving engine, batching Distill or fine-tune a small model to replace the big one on your task Adding more context "to be safe": cost grows with every token.
    Unreliable or unsafe outputs
    Hallucinates, breaks JSON, follows injected instructions    		 Structured outputs, validation, guardrails, grounding with citations Evals + LLM-as-judge in CI; human-in-the-loop for risky actions Fine-tune for refusal / format behaviour on your own red-team data Trusting a single model call for anything irreversible.
    Needs a new language or deep specialist knowledge
    Low-resource language, dense legal / medical / scientific domain    		 Pick a model already strong in it; RAG for the long tail Continued pre-training on a large domain corpus, then SFT Train from scratch Training from scratch unless you are a lab; it costs millions and the base models are better than you can make.

    The rule of thumb. Prompting changes nothing and costs nothing: try it first. RAG adds knowledge. Fine-tuning changes behaviour (style, format, reliability on a narrow task) and is bad at adding knowledge. Continued pre-training adds deep knowledge but needs a corpus and real GPU time. Pre-training from scratch is for labs.

    Who does what¶

    Role What they do Stages
    AI lab (OpenAI, Anthropic, Google, Meta, Mistral, DeepSeek…) Stages 1–4: collects data, designs the architecture, pre-trains, post-trains, publishes the model (closed API or open weights). 5, 6, 7, 8
    ML / research engineer Stage 5 and parts of 4: fine-tunes, distils, evaluates, runs training jobs on GPUs; writes PyTorch. 9, 8, 12
    Software / platform engineer Stages 6–7: serves or calls models, builds RAG, tools, agents and MCP servers, owns latency, cost, evals and observability. 10, 11, 13
    Data scientist / analyst Classic ML on tabular data (section 3), evaluation design, labelling guidelines, analysis of model outputs. 3, 12

    Stage numbers refer to glossary sections.
